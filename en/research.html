Research

Elucidating the principles of biological systems and diversification through epigenomics

Epigenomic information, such as DNA methylation and histone modifications, is a crucial layer of genetic information that defines the individuality of cells and organisms through the regulation of gene expression and chromosome function. Intriguingly, epigenomic information is also known to change dynamically in response to the intracellular and extracellular environment. In other words, the very blueprint of life itself changes dynamically. Our laboratory primarily uses plants to understand how epigenomic information is established and inherited, and how it changes in response to the environment. In particular, we take "epigenomics analysis"—the genome-wide profiling of epigenomic information—as our central approach, and by mastering both wet (experimental) and dry (computational) approaches, we tackle the mysteries of epigenomic regulation. Furthermore, by analyzing the dynamics of the epigenome under environmental changes, we explore the survival strategies of organisms—how they use the epigenome to adapt to their surroundings.

Main Research Themes

1. Elucidating the mechanisms of epigenome pattern formation and inheritance

Where and how abundantly epigenomic modifications, such as DNA methylation and histone modifications, are located in the genome varies among cells and individuals, and is known to change depending on intracellular and extracellular conditions. Epigenomic modifications are broadly controlled by three classes of proteins: "writers" that deposit the modifications, "erasers" that remove them, and "readers" that bind to and interpret the modifications. Using approaches from genetics, biochemistry, genomics, and computational biology, we aim to clarify how these proteins act to form the genome-wide epigenome pattern and how it is inherited through cell division and the reproductive process of the organism. So far, we have shown that the network woven by the interactions among modifications plays an important role in the formation and inheritance of the epigenome pattern, and we aim to further elucidate the functions of the proteins that govern each interaction within the network.

2. Elucidating the roles of the epigenome in development and environmental responses

We are interested in the molecular mechanisms that underlie the remarkable adaptive capacity of plants. Plants adapt to their constantly changing surroundings by dynamically altering not only their physiological functions but also their development. From the perspective of epigenomic regulation, we are advancing research to unravel the molecular mechanisms of this environmental adaptability in plants. In particular, we are interested in the phenomenon of "memory," in which plants remember past environmental changes and respond more rapidly to subsequent ones. Through our previous work, we have discovered mechanisms that "record" gene transcription in the form of epigenomic modifications. We aim to clarify how this recording mechanism contributes to the adaptive capacity of plants, and whether artificially rewriting this mechanism can further enhance their adaptability.

3. Understanding the mechanisms of epigenome diversification

Among the organisms on Earth, even within a single species such as Arabidopsis thaliana, there exists a wide variety of individuals and ecotypes, each carrying diverse traits. Behind this diversity lies not only the differences in genome sequence accumulated through evolution, but also the diversification of epigenomic modification patterns such as DNA methylation. We aim to understand how this diversification of epigenome patterns is brought about. Building on the modification networks that we have revealed as the basis for forming epigenome patterns, we will further develop this work to unravel the principles by which epigenome patterns diversify.
